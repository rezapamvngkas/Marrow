What you'll actually taste

We cup every batch before it ships. This is what keeps coming up.

Dark chocolate Up front, more cocoa powder than sweet bar. It carries through the whole cup, not just the first sip.
Brown sugar Comes from the Huila half of the blend. Rounds out the finish so the cup never turns sharp.
Blackberry Only shows up once the coffee's had a minute to cool. Brew a little coarser if you want more of it.
Roasted hazelnut Sits underneath everything else, most noticeable in the last third of the cup.

We started roasting because the diner's coffee was bad

Dana Voss ran the line at a breakfast spot in West Asheville for six years. The coffee coming out of the machine in back was thin and always a little burnt, so she started roasting six pounds at a time on a modified popcorn popper in the alley before her shift.

Customers started asking where the coffee at the counter came from. Three years and one real roaster later, Marrow ships to about forty states out of the same neighborhood, one blend at a time, roasted twice a week so it never sits around getting stale on a shelf.

We only make one thing. That means we get to actually know it.

However you make it at home

Marrow holds up across methods. Here's how we'd brew it, depending on the morning.

Espresso

Pull it short. The Huila's sugar handles the extra pressure well and keeps the shot from turning bitter.

Pour over

A medium grind and a slow pour brings the blackberry note forward. Our favorite way to drink it on a weekend.

French press

Go coarse and give it the full four minutes. Heavier body, less acidity, good for a cold morning.

Cold brew

Steep coarse grounds for sixteen hours. The chocolate note gets deeper and the acidity nearly disappears.

Questions we get asked

What roast is this?
Marrow No. 1 sits at medium-dark. It's roasted just past first crack, dark enough to hold up in espresso but not so dark that it loses the fruit in the Yirgacheffe.
Where are the beans sourced?
A washed lot from the Gedeo zone in Ethiopia and a natural-process lot from Huila, Colombia. We've bought from the same Huila farm since 2023.
Which brewing method works best?
It's built to work across methods, but pour over brings out the most of the blackberry note. Espresso and French press bring the chocolate and sugar forward instead.
What sizes are available?
Right now, just the 12 oz bag, whole bean or ground. We've stayed at one size on purpose, so nothing sits in a warehouse long enough to go stale.
How should I store the coffee?
Keep the bag sealed and out of direct light, ideally somewhere cool and dry. Skip the freezer, it does more harm than good once the bag's been opened.
